# Can You Use AI for a Civil Service Application? What Is Allowed in 2026

Yes, you can use AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini for a Civil Service application, within clear limits. The official Civil Service candidate guidance, checked in September 2026, allows AI to help you refine your ideas, research public information, structure your own examples and check spelling and clarity. It does not allow AI to invent or inflate your experience, write generic answers for you to copy, complete online tests, or produce answers during a live or pre recorded interview. ## Quick answer

Use AI to help you say it better, never to give you something to say. It can turn a job advert into a checklist, ask questions that help you remember your own evidence, check a draft against the behaviours for your grade, and tidy your grammar. Everything you submit must be true, accurate and taken from your own experience.

Keep AI completely out of three places: online tests such as the Civil Service Judgement Test, live interviews, and pre recorded video interviews. And never paste personal details, internal figures or confidential work information into a public AI tool.

Always read the official [Civil Service guidance on artificial intelligence and recruitment](https://www.civil-service-careers.gov.uk/artificial-intelligence-and-recruitment/) and your job advert before you start. If the advert says something different from this article, follow the advert.

## Key takeaways

- The Civil Service allows AI for preparation and polishing, not for inventing evidence or doing assessments for you.
- Applications where AI is used inappropriately may be rejected, and some guidance says internal candidates may face disciplinary action.
- Start with your own notes. Use AI to question you and review your draft, not to write it.
- Pitch every example at the grade in the advert, using the official Success Profiles behaviour examples.
- No AI during online tests, live interviews or pre recorded interviews.
- Read everything aloud before you submit. If a sentence could appear in anyone's application, rewrite it.

## What the Civil Service allows, and what it does not

This table summarises the official candidate guidance in plain English. Checked September 2026.

| Stage | Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Written application | Refining and clarifying your own ideas; researching public information about the department; relating industry trends to your own experience; structuring your own examples with STAR; checking spelling, grammar and clarity | Inventing or inflating skills and experience; creating generic answers and copying them in; giving AI sensitive information such as internal figures or personal details |
| Online tests | Practising beforehand with the official practice tests; building general skills before the test | Using AI to complete any assessment of personality or ability, including situational judgement and numerical tests |
| Interviews | Preparing with mock questions from the job description; practising behaviour questions; tips on communication | Creating answers during a live or pre recorded interview; creating answers to situation based questions; inventing experience |

The guiding idea is simple: the panel wants to find out about you. AI can help you present who you are. It cannot create someone who is not you.

## What happens if you get it wrong

Before you submit, you are asked to confirm that the information you provide is true and accurate. The Civil Service Careers guidance says applications may be rejected where AI is used inappropriately at any stage.

Some candidate guides and many job adverts go further. The [Scottish Government's candidate guide on AI](https://www.gov.scot/publications/recruitment-candidate-guide/pages/use-of-ai-tools/) says that where plagiarism is identified, including AI generated content presented as your own, applications will be withdrawn and internal candidates may be subject to disciplinary action.

There is also a practical reason. Generic, generated examples tend to score poorly anyway, because sift panels are looking for specific actions and results, not polished phrases.

## A safe method in four steps

### 1. Set the rules at the start of the chat

Tell the tool what it may and may not do before you give it anything:

> I am applying for a Civil Service job at [GRADE] level. Act as a coach, within the Civil Service guidance on AI in applications. Do not invent or add any experience, results, numbers or responsibilities. Do not write example answers for me to copy. Ask me questions, help me organise my own notes, and give me feedback on drafts I write. Use British English.

### 2. Bring your own evidence, made safe

Write rough notes about real situations first: what happened, what you did, and what changed. Remove names, case details and internal figures before pasting anything. Replace a colleague's name with "a new colleague", and exact internal numbers with honest general scale.

### 3. Let AI question you

> Here are my notes about something I did at work. [NOTES] Ask me up to eight questions, one at a time, to help me explain the situation, my own actions and reasons, and the result. Do not write the example.

Your answers are the evidence. "I'd need to check" is a good answer. Check it later.

### 4. Write it yourself, then ask for a review

> Here is my draft for [BEHAVIOUR] at [GRADE] level, limit [NUMBER] words, and the official examples of this behaviour at this grade: [PASTE]. Do not rewrite it. Tell me which parts match the grade, where I say "we" instead of "I", what is vague, whether the result is clear, and the word count.

Edit in your own words. Repeat once or twice. Then read it aloud.

## Pitch examples at the right grade

Civil Service recruitment uses [Success Profiles](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/success-profiles), and the behaviours are described differently for each group of grades. At EO, Delivering at Pace is about organising your own and a small team's work and checking progress. At HEO and SEO, it is about keeping a whole team focused on priorities and reassessing workloads when demands conflict. At Grade 7, examples move towards wider decisions and direction.

An excellent example pitched at the wrong grade scores poorly. Open the official behaviour examples for your grade and check that your draft shows them.

## Online tests and the Civil Service Judgement Test

Many campaigns include online tests, and the advert tells you which. The most common is the Civil Service Judgement Test. It has a short self assessment, then workplace scenarios where you rate each possible action as Counterproductive, Ineffective, Fairly Effective or Effective. GOV.UK says your answers must be your own and you must not ask anyone for input, and the Civil Service AI guidance rules out AI for these assessments. Hiring managers may ask interview candidates to retake the test under supervision.

Prepare with the official practice tests on [GOV.UK's Civil Service online tests page](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/civil-service-online-tests), then close every AI tool before you start the real test.

## Pre recorded interviews

Pre recorded video interviews are common for AO and EO roles: questions appear on screen and you record your answers against a timer, often with only one attempt. AI can help you practise beforehand, for example by generating mock questions from the job description. Nothing AI generated should be on your screen or desk while you record, and no AI tool should be open.

## Mistakes that cost candidates

**Asking AI to "write a Leadership example".** You get something generic that is not your evidence.

**Accepting a rewrite because it sounds better.** Polished is not the same as specific. Check every fact survived.

**Adding numbers you cannot defend.** You may be asked about them at interview.

**Pasting work documents or case details.** Summarise in your own general words instead.

**Using the same generated phrases as everyone else.** Leveraged, spearheaded, passionate, dynamic. Panels notice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use ChatGPT for my Civil Service application?

Yes, for permitted tasks. The official Civil Service guidance allows AI tools, including ChatGPT, to help refine your ideas, research public information, structure your own examples and check spelling and grammar. You must not use it to invent or exaggerate experience or to create generic answers you copy into the form. Everything you submit must be true and your own.

### Can I use AI to write my Civil Service behaviour examples?

Not to write them from nothing. You can use AI to question you about your own experience, help you structure your notes with STAR, and review your draft against the behaviour examples for your grade. The example itself must describe what you actually did, in your own words. A generic generated example breaks the guidance and usually scores poorly.

### Can I use AI for the Civil Service Judgement Test?

No. The Civil Service guidance lists completing assessments of personality and ability, including situational judgement tests, as an unacceptable use of AI, and GOV.UK says test answers must be your own. You can prepare beforehand with the official practice test and by learning how the behaviours work at your grade.

### Can I use AI during a pre recorded interview?

No. The guidance says AI must not be used to create answers during a live or pre recorded interview, or in any live assessment. You can use AI before the interview to practise with mock questions, then close it before you start recording.

### Will the Civil Service know if I used AI?

Do not plan around detection. The questions to ask are whether every sentence is true, whether it is yours, and whether you can explain it at interview. Experienced panels often recognise generic generated text, and you confirm that your application is true and accurate when you submit it.

### Is it acceptable to use AI for spelling and grammar?

Yes. Checking spelling, grammar and clarity is listed as acceptable use in the official guidance. Ask the tool to list suggested changes rather than rewrite your text, so you can check that none of them changes your meaning.
