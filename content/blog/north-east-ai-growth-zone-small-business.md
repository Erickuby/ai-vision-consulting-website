# What the North East AI Growth Zone Means for Small Businesses

The North East AI Growth Zone is a regional programme covering Newcastle, Gateshead, Sunderland, North Tyneside, South Tyneside, Northumberland and County Durham. It is designed to attract data centre investment, build AI skills locally and help organisations adopt AI. For most small businesses in the region, the practical opportunity is not a grant cheque arriving in the post. It is cheaper skills, better local suppliers, new contracts around construction and operations, and a stronger reason to fix the boring workflows that already cost you hours every week.

## Quick answer

The North East AI Growth Zone is a designation, not a payout. It funnels investment and skills funding into the region, with the North East Combined Authority reporting the potential for more than 5,000 new jobs and QTS and Blackstone committing around 10 billion pounds to a data centre campus at Cambois in Blyth.

A Newcastle small business should respond in four steps:

1. Decide whether you are a supplier to the build, a user of AI, or both.
2. Use the funded skills routes before paying for training.
3. Automate one workflow that already wastes staff time.
4. Make your business findable for the searches that will grow around AI work in the region.

Ignore anything that promises a fast Growth Zone grant for buying software.

## Key takeaways

- The Growth Zone covers seven local authority areas, so most North East businesses are inside it.
- The headline money is infrastructure investment, not a general small business fund.
- Skills funding is the part you can use soonest, including Skills Bootcamps and Growth Hub support.
- Supply chain work around construction, facilities, security, logistics and hospitality is a realistic route in.
- Adopting AI in your own operations is still the fastest return for most firms.
- Treat unsolicited offers of Growth Zone funding with caution and verify through official channels.

## What the North East AI Growth Zone actually is

An AI Growth Zone is a government designation intended to speed up the things that normally slow large computing projects down, including planning decisions and grid connections. The North East was designated in 2025, and the North East Combined Authority is coordinating delivery across Durham, Gateshead, Newcastle, North Tyneside, Northumberland, South Tyneside and Sunderland.

The programme is usually described around four pillars:

- skills, so local people can take AI-related jobs
- adoption, so businesses and public services can use AI safely
- innovation, supporting research, start-ups and new products
- infrastructure, covering data, energy and connectivity

The anchor project is the QTS Cambois data centre campus in Blyth, backed by Blackstone with a commitment reported at around 10 billion pounds. Existing capacity at Cobalt Park in North Tyneside also sits inside the picture.

Alongside the zone, the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology announced a regional skills push in May 2026, including 1.5 million pounds of government investment through TechFirst and 750,000 pounds from the North East mayor. The stated aims include AI and digital skills for around 30,000 primary school children, benefits for 80,000 students by 2029, support for 1,000 teachers and 150 local work placements.

Those numbers matter for a small business in one narrow way. They tell you where the region is spending attention, which is where local demand, local talent and local contracts will follow.

## What it does not mean for your business

Three honest clarifications, because there is a lot of noise locally.

**It is not a small business grant scheme.** The large figures relate to private infrastructure investment and skills programmes. They are not a fund you apply to for a chatbot or a new laptop.

**It does not guarantee jobs in your postcode.** Data centres are capital intensive and employ fewer people per pound invested than most sectors. Construction phases create more local work than steady-state operations. Plan around phases, not headlines.

**It does not change your obligations.** Data protection, employment law and consumer rules apply exactly as before. A Growth Zone designation does not loosen anything, and the Information Commissioner's Office guidance on AI and data protection still applies to how you handle personal data.

## Where the opportunity is real

### 1. Supply chain work around the build

Large campus projects need groundworks, fencing, security, cleaning, catering, waste, plant hire, temporary accommodation, signage, print, occupational health, driver hire and dozens of similar services. These are ordinary North East businesses, not AI companies.

If that is you, the useful work is unglamorous: register on the relevant supplier portals, get your accreditations current, make sure your insurance and health and safety documentation is ready to submit at short notice, and be able to answer a prequalification questionnaire without a two week scramble.

### 2. Cheaper skills for your existing team

Skills Bootcamps in the North East are free, flexible courses of up to sixteen weeks delivered with employers and training providers. The current phase was backed by 26 million pounds from the Department for Education, with a target of helping more than 8,000 people into work in growing industries. Digital and creative is one of the subject areas.

The Business and IP Centre in Newcastle also runs free practical AI workshops for small businesses, and businesses in Newcastle, Gateshead and Sunderland may be eligible for funded advisory hours through local business support. Check current eligibility before you plan around it, because programme windows change.

### 3. Adoption inside your own business

This is the part you control. Most small firms in the region are not going to build AI products. They are going to save four to six hours a week by fixing quoting, enquiry handling, scheduling and reporting.

That work is available right now, does not depend on any announcement, and compounds. If you want a structured way to choose the first one, read our guide on [what a small business should automate first](/blog/what-to-automate-first-small-business).

### 4. Recruitment and retention

AI literacy is now near the top of the skills employers say they want, and the number of North East roles mentioning AI skills has risen sharply. If your competitors are advertising AI-aware roles and you are not, you will feel it in hiring.

You do not need to hire a data scientist. You need people who can use tools competently and judge the output critically.

## A practical ninety day plan

### Days 1 to 14: decide your position

Write one sentence describing your route: supplier, adopter, or both. Vague ambition produces vague action. If you cannot name the contract type or the workflow, you are not ready to spend money.

### Days 15 to 30: fix the evidence

Suppliers need current insurance, accreditations, references, policies and a short capability statement. Adopters need a list of the five tasks that consume the most staff time, with rough hours attached. Both need someone named as owner.

### Days 31 to 60: use the funded routes first

Check Skills Bootcamps, free workshops and any funded advisory hours you qualify for before paying for training. Send two people rather than one, because a single trained person becomes a bottleneck and often leaves.

### Days 61 to 90: run one small change

Pick a single workflow, run the new method alongside the old one, and measure hours saved and errors caught. Keep a person responsible for exceptions. Then decide whether to expand it or stop.

Do not run a transformation programme. Run one change, prove it, repeat.

## Risks worth taking seriously

- **Grant scams.** Expect cold calls and emails referencing the Growth Zone. Verify anything financial through the North East Combined Authority or the North East Growth Hub directly, not through a link in an email.
- **Software bought before the problem is defined.** Tools are cheap to buy and expensive to unwind. Define the workflow first.
- **Consultancy sold on hype.** If a proposal cannot state what changes, who owns it and how success is measured, it is not a proposal.
- **Data handling shortcuts.** Putting customer data into unvetted tools is a real risk. Decide what may be shared, with which tool, by whom.
- **Energy and planning assumptions.** Large infrastructure timelines slip. Do not build a business plan that depends on a specific opening date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can my small business apply for AI Growth Zone funding?

There is no general small business grant attached to the designation itself. The realistic routes are skills funding such as Skills Bootcamps, existing business support programmes through the North East Growth Hub, and commercial supply chain work. Check eligibility directly with the Combined Authority or Growth Hub, because programmes open and close.

### Does the Growth Zone only benefit Blyth and Northumberland?

No. The Growth Zone spans seven local authority areas including Newcastle, Gateshead and Sunderland. The largest single construction project sits at Cambois in Blyth, but skills funding, supply chain demand and adoption support apply across the region.

### Will the data centres create many local jobs?

The North East Combined Authority has referenced the potential for more than 5,000 jobs across the Growth Zone, and QTS has spoken about creating more than a thousand roles. Construction phases typically generate more local employment than ongoing operations, so timing matters when you plan.

### Do I need to buy AI software to benefit?

No. Many businesses gain more from training two staff properly and fixing one workflow than from new software. Buy tools once you can describe the task, the owner and the measure of success.

### How do I avoid Growth Zone related scams?

Treat unsolicited funding offers with suspicion, especially those asking for fees, bank details or urgent decisions. Verify through official North East Combined Authority and North East Growth Hub channels, and never act on a link in an unexpected email.
