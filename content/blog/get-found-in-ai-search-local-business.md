# How to Get Your Local Business Found in AI Search

More customers now start with a question to an assistant rather than a search box. They ask for a plumber in Gateshead, a bookkeeper in Sunderland or an AI trainer in Newcastle, and they act on the two or three names that come back. This guide explains what genuinely influences whether your business is one of those names, what the platforms themselves say, and which popular advice is not supported by the documentation.

## Quick answer

There is no secret setting that gets your business into an AI answer. Assistants build local answers from the same public material that search engines use: your website, your Google Business Profile and other listings, reviews, and third party pages that mention you.

Focus on five things:

1. Make your services, locations and prices readable as plain text on your own site.
2. Keep your business name, address and phone number identical everywhere.
3. Complete your Google Business Profile properly, especially the primary category.
4. Earn steady, genuine reviews rather than occasional bursts.
5. Get mentioned on pages other people control, such as directories, press and partner sites.

Google states directly that there are no special optimisations or extra structured data required to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Standard indexable, helpful content is the requirement.

## Key takeaways

- Assistants cannot recommend a business they cannot read about in text.
- Consistency of name, address and phone number matters more than clever wording.
- Your Google Business Profile primary category is the single most influential listing field.
- Reviews influence prominence, and steady flow beats sudden bursts.
- Third party mentions matter because assistants draw on sources you do not own.
- Blocking crawlers or hiding key details in images removes you from the answer entirely.

## Why this changed

Local discovery used to end in a map pack. Now a large share of local queries get an assistant answer first, whether that is Google AI Overviews, Gemini, ChatGPT or Perplexity. The answer usually names a small number of businesses and summarises why.

That has two consequences for a small business. Fewer people scroll, so being mentioned matters more than being ranked tenth. And the assistant is summarising sources rather than reading your marketing, so what other people publish about you carries real weight.

Treat this as an extension of local search rather than a separate discipline. The inputs overlap heavily.

## What actually influences whether you get named

### 1. Text a machine can read

Assistants work from text. If your services, service area, opening hours and prices only exist inside an image, a PDF or a video, they may not be used.

Make sure each of these appears as ordinary text on your site:

- what you do, in the words customers use
- the towns and areas you serve
- how you charge, even as a range or a starting point
- how to contact you and how quickly you reply
- who you are, with a named person behind the business

A page that says "trusted solutions for modern businesses" tells an assistant nothing. A page that says "bookkeeping for sole traders and limited companies in Newcastle, Gateshead and North Tyneside, from 95 pounds per month" is usable.

### 2. Your Google Business Profile

Google describes local ranking as relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance. You can change relevance by completing the profile properly, and prominence by being genuinely well known.

Priorities in order:

- choose the correct primary category, because a mismatch is costly
- add relevant secondary categories without padding
- list services individually rather than as one blob
- keep opening hours, holiday hours and contact details accurate
- add real photographs, refreshed occasionally
- answer questions on the profile in your own words

### 3. Name, address and phone consistency

Assistants and search engines cross check listings. If your address is abbreviated differently across your site, a directory and your invoices, that weakens confidence in the record.

Pick one canonical format and use it everywhere, including the trading name, unit or suite, street, town, postcode and phone number.

### 4. Reviews

Reviews affect both the decision to recommend and the reason given. Volume, recency, rating and the language customers use all feed the summary.

Ask every satisfied customer, at the moment the work is finished, using a direct link. Reply to reviews, including the difficult ones, and never buy or incentivise them.

### 5. Sources you do not own

This is the part most small businesses neglect. Assistants pull from directories, local news, professional bodies, chambers of commerce, partner sites, community pages and forums.

Practical actions:

- claim listings on the directories that matter in your sector
- get listed by trade bodies and accreditation schemes you belong to
- pitch one useful local story a quarter rather than generic press releases
- ask partners and suppliers to describe what you do accurately on their sites
- participate genuinely in community discussion rather than posting promotion

### 6. Technical basics

If a crawler cannot fetch the page, nothing else matters. Check that your site is indexable, that robots.txt is not blocking important paths, and that you are not applying nosnippet or noindex to pages you want used. Google confirms those existing controls also govern AI features.

## What does not work

| Common advice | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| Add an llms.txt file so AI can read your site | Google states no new machine readable or AI text files are needed |
| Add special AI schema markup | Google states no special structured data is required for AI features |
| Stuff town names across every page | Thin location pages read as spam and help nobody |
| Buy reviews to lift the average | Against platform policy, detectable, and damaging when found |
| Publish daily thin blog posts | Volume without substance does not create prominence |
| Pay for guaranteed inclusion in ChatGPT answers | No such placement exists to sell |

Structured data still has value for eligibility for rich results in normal search, and it makes your facts unambiguous. It is simply not a magic entry ticket to an assistant answer.

## A four week plan for a local business

### Week 1: audit what a machine can see

Search for your own business name, then for the service plus town phrase you want. Ask an assistant the question a customer would ask. Note whether you appear, who does, and what the answer says about them.

Read your homepage and main service page as if you knew nothing about the business. Write down every question you cannot answer from the text.

### Week 2: fix your own pages

Rewrite the service page so it states the service, the area, the price shape, the process and the contact route in plain text. Add a short frequently asked questions section using real customer questions. Add a page for each distinct service rather than one page listing everything.

### Week 3: fix your listings

Complete the Google Business Profile fully. Correct name, address and phone details across every listing you can find. Remove duplicate listings. Add services and photographs.

### Week 4: build outside signals

Request reviews from recent customers. Claim two relevant directories. Contact one partner and one local publication with something genuinely useful. Set a monthly reminder so this does not become a one off.

## How to measure it

Assistant visibility is harder to measure than rankings, so keep it simple.

- Track branded and unbranded enquiries and ask new customers how they found you.
- Watch Google Search Console performance, where Google reports that traffic from AI features appears under the standard web search type.
- Check Google Business Profile calls, direction requests and website clicks month on month.
- Repeat the same three assistant questions monthly and record whether you are named.

Do not expect a clean attribution line. Look for direction of travel across several measures.

## Data and honesty cautions

Be careful with your customers' data and your own claims. If you use AI tools to draft content or handle enquiries, do not paste personal data into tools you have not assessed, and follow the Information Commissioner's Office guidance on AI and data protection.

Equally, do not let a tool invent credentials, awards, accreditations or review counts. Assistants repeat what they find. A false claim on your own site becomes a false claim in somebody else's answer, and that is a trust problem you cannot easily undo.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an llms.txt file for AI search?

No. Google states that you do not need to create new machine readable files, AI text files or markup to appear in its AI features. Some sites publish one anyway as a courtesy, but treat it as optional and low priority next to readable content, accurate listings and reviews.

### Does schema markup help me appear in AI answers?

Google says no special structured data is required for AI Overviews or AI Mode. Structured data remains useful for standard rich results and for stating your facts unambiguously, so it is worth having, but it is not the lever that decides whether an assistant names you.

### Why does ChatGPT recommend my competitor and not me?

Usually because more readable, third party evidence exists about them. Check whether your services and service area are stated in plain text, whether your listings are consistent, whether you have recent reviews, and whether other sites describe you accurately.

### Should I block AI crawlers from my website?

Only if you have a specific reason, and understand the trade-off. Blocking reduces the chance of being represented in answers. If you publish paid or proprietary material, restrict that content specifically rather than blocking the pages that sell your services.

### How long does it take to see a difference?

Listing corrections can register within weeks. Reviews and third party mentions build over months. Treat it as a quarterly rhythm rather than a campaign, and expect the earliest visible change in calls and direction requests rather than in assistant answers.
